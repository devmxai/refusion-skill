The project as one JSON document: every layer by its key, its time, its values, its motion, its animators and edges, every scene by its name. The engine checks everything first and gives every refusal at once by its address; nothing is written when there is one. Then it changes only what differs, in one history step.

It is reached by one of two doors, with the same form and the same checks:

- **In the project's folder, on a desktop:** the document is the file `project.rfp`. Write it by hand and save it; that is the only way you write the project there (no script, and no writing tool for what the file carries). The section *The project's file* below says how.
- **Through the connector, with no folder** (the phone, a remote assistant): the same file, read with `project.file_read` and written back whole with `project.file_write`, as the next section says.

## Through the connector: read, write, look, mend

1. **Read** the file: `project.file_read` (`file` `project.rfp`, or a scene's own file `scenes/<name>.rfs`). Its `text` is what you edit and write back; with `address` (`@scene`, `@key`, `@scene/key`) it reads one scene or one layer in the same form.
2. **Write** it whole with `project.file_write` (`file`, `text`): `{"refusion": "project", "version": 2, "written": ..., "canvas": [w, h], "fps": n, "for": "15s", "layers": [...], "scenes": [...]}`, `written` kept as you read it. A large change first with `"plan": true` in the head: the reply says what would change and every refusal, and applies nothing until you take that line away and write again.
3. **Read the reply:** `applied`; `changes`; `errors`, each at its `line` and `column` with its `code`, its `words` and a ready `fix` where the engine has one (mend those lines only and write again); `remarks` (what it did that you did not say in so many words: a time put on the frame grid, a key on a layer's end taken on its last frame); `notes` (what the engine reads in what changed: a jump, a box off the canvas, a costly blur); and a picture of what changed.
4. **Look** before you say it is done: `render.look`, `render.strip`, `motion.trace`, `scene.layout`, `render.frame`. Mend by address.

## The form

- **A layer** is named by its `key` (lower-case letters, digits and `_`, from a letter), or by its `id` for one with no key. A key the composition lacks makes the layer: give its `kind`, `at` and `for`. The list runs from the bottom up: the order written is the order drawn.
- **A value you give is set; a value you leave out stays** on a layer that is there, and is a new layer's default on one you make. Only what differs is written, so a document read and written back changes nothing.
- **Values:** a number; a pair `[x, y]`; a colour `#RRGGBB`, `#RRGGBBAA` or `[r, g, b, a]` from 0 to 1; a choice by its name (`"Bold"`, `"Ellipse"`, `"Center"`); a text; `true`/`false`.
- **Times:** `"1.25s"`, `"75f"` (frames of the project's rate) or `{"num": 5, "den": 4}`. A layer's `at` and `for` are in the time of the composition the layer is in; **every time inside a layer is counted from its `at`** (version 2): its motions' keys, the key times `settle` and `tangents` name, its accents. So moving `at` moves the layer with all its motion, and a motion that starts as the layer does is at `"0s"`. A time that differs from what is there lands on the frame grid (`snapped` counts them); write times on frames and none moves. A document of version 1 (keys in the composition's time) is still read, each layer's keys counted from its `at`, and the reply says so; a project's file of version 1 is written again as version 2 at its places.
- **Motion:** `{"from": a, "to": b, "at": "0.5s", "for": "0.4s", "ease": "ease_out"}` for two keys, `{"keys": [["0s", a, "smooth"], ["0.5s", b, "hold"], ["1s", c]]}` for more (the last key has no ease). A plain value on a property that moves takes its keys away.
- **Ease:** `linear` (or none), `hold`, a curve by name (`smooth`, `in_out_cubic`, `in_out_quart`, `in_out_quint`, `in_out_expo`, `whip`, `spin_build`, `out_expo`, `in_expo`, `out_back`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `brake`, `in_sine`, `out_sine`, `in_out_sine`, `in_quad`, `out_quad`, `in_out_quad`, `in_cubic`, `out_cubic`, `in_quart`, `out_quart`, `in_quint`, `out_quint`, `in_circ`, `out_circ`, `in_out_circ`, `in_back`, `in_out_back`), a kept shape (`spring`, `bounce`, `elastic`, `spring_in`, `bounce_in`, `elastic_in`, `spring_in_out`, `bounce_in_out`, `elastic_in_out`): a bounce or an elastic band with its liveliness from 0.05 to 1 (`bounce(0.5)`), a spring with its two numbers (`spring(bounce 0.2, visual 0.5)`, below), or `cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2)`. The curves bear the industry's names (CSS, GSAP, After Effects), by the role a move plays: an arrival is `out_` (quick to start, soft to land: `out_expo`, `out_quart`, `out_cubic`; `out_back` lands a little past and comes back), a leaving is `in_` (slow to start, gone fast: `in_expo`, `in_cubic`; `in_back` draws back first), a move across the frame is `in_out_` (soft at both ends: `in_out_cubic`, `in_out_quart`). An earlier name is still read as the curve with its numbers, and the reply names it (`expo` is `in_out_expo`, `settle` is `out_expo`, `gather` is `in_expo`, `overshoot` is `out_back`, `cinematic_soft` is `in_out_cubic`, `cinematic` is `in_out_quart`, `cinematic_hard` is `in_out_quint`).
- **A spring's two numbers:** `spring(bounce 0.2, visual 0.5)` (and `spring_in`, `spring_in_out`): `bounce` how much it bounces, 0 (none: it lands without coming back) to 0.9; `visual` the share of the segment its bulk takes, 0.05 to 1. The two are apart, as Apple's and Material's springs keep them: a slow spring that bounces and a quick one that does not are both written. A spring still wobbling more than 0.1 % of the way at its segment's end (the envelope of its wobble, as the industry's elastic ease leaves it) is refused, since the motion would stop while it moves: give it a smaller `visual` or less `bounce`; the refusal names the largest `visual` that settles. An older `spring(0.6)`, a liveliness, is read as the spring it is, exactly, and said in the reply. The ready springs the app's ease sheet gives, each settled: calm `spring(bounce 0, visual 0.5)`, normal `spring(bounce 0.15, visual 0.5)`, lively `spring(bounce 0.3, visual 0.5)`.
- **A settle after a key (a physical landing):** a motion's `settle` says what the value does once it has arrived, by physics: it carries on the speed the motion arrives with, so a faster arrival goes further, and the arrival keeps all its own time. `"settle": "impact(elasticity 0.4, hop 0.2s)"` is a ball: it hits the key and hops back the way it came, each hop leaving at `elasticity` of the speed it hit with (0.05 to 0.9), under a gravity that makes the first hop last `hop` (0.05 to 1 s); it never crosses the key. `"settle": "overshoot(frequency 3, decay 6)"` is inertia: it goes on past the key, comes back and dies away (`frequency` swings a second, 0.5 to 8; `decay` a second, 1 to 30). On a motion of two keys it is the last key's; on keyed motion it is a map from a key's time, as the keys' times are written, to its settle (`"settle": {"1.2s": "impact(elasticity 0.4, hop 0.2s)"}`). It moves a number or a position, only while the value rests after its key (the last key, or one the next segment holds or keeps still), and ends on a rest of its own (a landing, a half swing) once within 0.1 % of the way the motion came, before the next key, or it is refused with the time it needs. For a landing that bounces, let the arrival keep its speed: an arrival that ends with no speed (an `out_` curve, a spring) has nothing to carry on and the settle moves nothing (the reply says so). The curves that arrive with speed: `linear`, `in_expo`, `ease_in`, `in_sine`, `in_quad`, `in_cubic`, `in_quart`, `in_quint`, `in_circ`, `in_back`. One tool sets it for any client: `anim.set_key_settle`. The app's ease sheet gives two ready landings in one step (`anim.set_key_landing`), and a document reads them as their two parts: a drop is `in_quad` (a fall from rest) with an `impact` whose hop is 2 × elasticity × the fall's time, so the bounces keep the fall's gravity (`"ease": "in_quad", "settle": "impact(elasticity 0.45, hop 0.36s)"` for a fall of 0.4 s); an inertia is `in_sine` with an `overshoot` of one stiffness.
- **A role's settle:** the overshoot `"ease": "move"` makes in the expressive system goes 1.5% of its last segment's way past, whatever that segment's length, so a short move may get a stiffer settle than a hand can set: `frequency` up to 120 and `decay` up to 1000 (from 0.5 and 1). The file writes it back as the role; once the motion is no longer the role's, its settle is written with those numbers and read again as it is. `anim.set_key_settle` and the rulers stay in the hand's ranges above.
- **A back with its share:** `out_back(15%)` lands that share of the way past and comes back, `in_back(8%)` draws that far back before it goes (the wind-up), `in_out_back(10%)` does both; from 1 % to 40 %, to the hundredth. Each is solved exactly as a Bézier of its family and read back by the same name; the plain `out_back` goes 9.78 % past. It is an ease of keys and a channel's `travel` or `settle` (on a channel up to what its handles hold); a phase's `function` takes the named curves.
- **A text's animators:** `"text_motion": {"in": {...}, "out": {...}, "loop": {...}}`, each with `preset` (in and out: `fade`, `pop`, `slide`, `rise`, `track`; loop: `wave`, `wiggle`, `pulse`, `sway`, `flicker`, `jitter`), `unit` (`All`, `Word`, `Letter`, `Line`), `duration` (in and out 0.05 s to 5 s; a loop's period 0.25 s to 10 s), `stagger` (0 s to 1 s), `span` (0.2 s to 30 s), `function` (in and out, and a layer's edges: `linear`, `smooth`, `out_expo`, `out_back`, `spring_soft`, `spring_snappy`, `in_out_cubic`, `in_out_quart`, `in_out_quint`, `in_out_expo`, `whip`, `spin_build`, `in_expo`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `brake`, `in_sine`, `out_sine`, `in_out_sine`, `in_quad`, `out_quad`, `in_out_quad`, `in_cubic`, `out_cubic`, `in_quart`, `out_quart`, `in_quint`, `out_quint`, `in_circ`, `out_circ`, `in_out_circ`, `in_back`, `in_out_back`, `step`; a loop: `sine`, `noise`), `order` (`Forward`, `Reverse`, `Middle out`, `Ends in`, `Random`, `On screen`), `strength` (a loop's step), `tint`, `stroke_tint`, `stroke_width`, `clip` (`true`: each unit rises from behind its line's edge), `caret`, and the away numbers that replace the preset's: `angle` (0 to 360: the compass direction the unit comes from, in degrees (0 from above, 90 from the right, 180 from below, 270 from the left)); `distance` (0 to 8: how far from rest it starts, in ems of the text's size); `scale_from` (0.05 to 2: how large it starts, as a multiple of its own size); `turn_from` (-720 to 720: how far turned it starts, in degrees); `skew_from` (-60 to 60: how far it leans at the start, in degrees); `blur_from` (0 to 0.6: how blurred it starts, in ems of the text's size). A layer's own `edges` take the first four of them. A phase that differs is set again; `null` takes it away; one not given stays.
- **A layer's own edges:** `"edges": {"in": {...}, "out": {...}, "loop": {...}}`, each with `preset` (in and out: the presets above; loop: `wave`, `wiggle`, `pulse`, `sway`, `flicker`, `jitter`), `function`, `duration`, the away numbers for in and out, `strength` and `hold` (times a second, 0 smooth) for the loop.
- **Channels** (in and out of `text_motion` and `edges`): `"channels": {"move": {"until": 0.25, "reach": 0.81, "travel": "linear", "settle": "brake"}, "fade": {"until": 0.4, "travel": "smooth"}}` gives each named channel a pace of its own over the phase's turn, so the opacity is done early while the move crosses its way and settles. The channels: `move`, `size`, `turn`, `lean`, `blur`, `fade` (a layer's own edges: `move`, `size`, `turn`, `fade`). Each takes `wait` (the share of the turn spent still first, 0 to 0.8; absent 0), `until` (where the travel ends, 0.1 to 1, at least 0.05 after `wait`; absent 1), `reach` (how far along the way it is then, 0.5 to 1.5: more goes past and settles back; with `until` 1 it is 1; absent 1), `travel` (its curve: any curve by name, `brake` too, or `cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2)` with y from -1 to 2; absent: the phase's own `function`) and `settle` (the curve from there to rest; absent `smooth`). Given, `channels` is what they are: a channel it leaves out follows the phase's curve, and `{}` takes them all away; not given, a phase set again keeps the channels and the library item it had.
- **Recipes** (in and out of `text_motion` and `edges`): `"recipe": "snap_in_from_the_left"` makes the phase a ready-made motion of the library whole, as the inspector applies it: its preset, its numbers and its channels, and the library item it came from; `preset` may then be left out. The fields given beside it go over it; a field left out keeps the recipe's. Read, a phase names the recipe it came from, modified or not, with its numbers after it; a phase that is its recipe already is not applied again, so what the file leaves out stays, the owner's changes too. A text's: in `words_pop`, `letters_rise`, `letters_fade`, `typewriter`, `slide_in_from_the_left`, `slide_in_from_the_right`, `drop_in`, `zoom_in`, `spin_in`, `centre_out`, `track_in`, `letters_rise_from_a_blur`, `lean_in_from_the_left`, `letters_snap_up_from_a_blur`, `snap_in_leaning_from_the_left`, `words_bounce_in`; out `words_pop_out`, `letters_fade_out`, `slide_out_to_the_left`, `slide_out_to_the_right`, `zoom_out`, `gather_out_to_the_left`. A layer's edges: in `slide_in_from_the_left`, `slide_in_from_the_right`, `drop_in`, `rise_in`, `zoom_in`, `spin_in`, `snap_in_from_the_left`, `snap_in_from_the_right`, `bounce_in`; out `slide_out_to_the_left`, `slide_out_to_the_right`, `drop_out`, `zoom_out`, `spin_out`, `gather_out_to_the_left`.
- **Media:** `"kind": "video"` (or `audio`) with `source`, the name of a file of the project's library (`media.library_snapshot`), and `trim_in`, where it starts in the file. A picture is a shape painted with it: `"kind": "shape"` with `fill_source`. A scene is shown by `"kind": "scene"` with `scene` and `offset`; its `transform.position` is where the scene's top-left corner goes (a card of 600 by 760 centred at [420, 540] is at [120, 160]).
- **Scenes:** `"scenes": [{"name": "Lower third", "canvas": [900, 220], "for": "5s", "layers": [...]}]`; a name the project lacks makes the scene, before any layer, so a layer of any composition can show it.
- **Addresses:** `@key.path`, `@12.path` for a layer with no key, `@scene/key.path`, a layer's own field (`@title.at`, `@title.for`, `@title.parent`) and a motion's part (`.from`, `.to`, `.at`, `.for`, `.ease` of a motion of two keys).
- **`omitted`** in what you read counts what the document does not carry yet. It is read, never written; what it counts stays as it is when you write.

## From what you mean to what you write

Every name here is the engine's (the page `kinds` lists every value of every kind, with its range and its names). Write these before keys by hand:

- **Rounded corners:** `"shape.radius": 24` sets a rectangle's four corners at once (`"full"` makes a pill at any size); a corner written beside it (`shape.corner_bl`) is kept. The engine writes four equal corners back as one radius. A scene's length is its `for`, changed from the file like any value.
- **What moves, fades or is arranged together:** a group, `"kind": "group"` with its `layers` inside it, placed from the group's top left and timed from its start. Fade or move the group, not each layer: it is drawn as one picture, so overlapping layers fade as one and keep their order. A group's `layout` stands its layers in a row or a column. Keep its layers at zero or more inside it: what lies left of or above its top left is cut off.
- **Things that arrive one after another (a chat, a list that grows, a feed):** give the layout `"moves": {"for": "0.4s", "ease": "out_cubic"}`: an item takes room only while it shows, and when one comes or goes the others ease to their new places. A chat that grows upward is a column with `"justify": "end"`: each new message pushes the ones before it up. Give each item its own `at`; its entrance (`edges`) brings it in.
- **Things in a grid (a wall of photos, a menu of cards, figures side by side):** `"layout": {"stack": "grid", "columns": 3, "gap": 24}` stands them in cells row by row; give an item `"size": ["fill", "hug"]` to take its cell's width. Change `columns` and the whole grid stands again: write no position for its items.
- **Things in a row or a column:** give the scene a `layout` (`"stack": "row", "gap": 24, "align": "center", "justify": "center"`) and write no position for its items: the engine stands them one after the other at their own sizes, so a longer text or a larger card moves the ones after it. A layer that should stay free is left out of `items`, or pinned.
- **Sized by what it holds or where it stands, not by numbers:** a chat bubble or a pill is one text with its backdrop (`backdrop.mode` `Box`, its padding and radius in ems): it is as large as its words. `"size_max": [640, null]` wraps a longer text at 640 and lets it grow down, so in a column the bubbles after it move. In a layout, `"size": ["fill", "hug"]` takes the room left (a bar as wide as the column, a spacer in a row); write no `shape.size` for a shape the engine sizes on both axes.
- **Measuring a text before placing by it:** `text.layout_snapshot` on a text layer gives its box, its lines and its words as the picture lays them out; with `text` it measures other words in the layer's style (how wide, how many lines), and with `carets` (`3 7 12`) where a caret stands after that many characters: one reading for every partial text of a typed line, with no layer made to probe.
- **What `transform.position` names (REFUSION_PLAN DF-86):** for a shape, its middle; for a text, the left edge of its box, by the top of its first line, whichever way its lines read, so a text whose position is the canvas's middle starts there and runs to the right. To centre a text, or to place it by an edge, pin it (`"pin": {"center_x": 0}`) or stand it in a layout; any other layer's point is its `transform.anchor`.
- **Placed by what it is next to, not by numbers:** `"pin": {"center": "@badge"}` centres a label in its pill whatever the label's length; `"pin": {"top": "@card.bottom + 16", "left": "@card"}` puts a caption under a card, its left edge on the card's; `"pin": {"right": 48, "bottom": 48}` keeps a logo in the corner of its container. Write no `transform.position` for a layer pinned on both axes: the engine places it at every instant, so a longer text or a card that moves takes it along.
- **A card made once, placed many times:** make the card a scene and declare it a template: its `template` names the values each copy changes (`controls`: a text, a picture, a colour, a switch), each on a layer of the scene; a picture's place is a shape with a `slot`. Place the scene as many times as you need (`"kind": "scene"`), each with its own `values`: one design, the copies changing only what you said.
- **A field of dots, a grid, stripes, grain:** `"fill.kind": "Pattern"` with `fill.pattern_kind` (`Checker`, `Grid`, `Dots`, `Stripes`, `Noise`, `Grain`), `fill.pattern_scale`, `fill.pattern_ink`, `fill.pattern_ground`; a printed look over a picture is the effect `stylize.halftone`.
- **A living background:** `"fill.kind": "Evolving field"` with `fill.field_kind` (`Noise`, `Cells`, `Waves`, `Rays`) and `fill.field_phase` moving; or `"Colour points"` with `fill.point_color`, `fill.point_position` moving; or `"Mesh gradient"`.
- **A line that draws itself:** a shape's stroke (`stroke.width`, `stroke.color`) with `stroke.trim_end` moving from 0 to 100.
- **Words in one by one:** `text_motion.in` with `unit` `Word` (or `Letter`, `Line`), a `preset` and a `stagger`.
- **A shape that turns into another:** `shape.morph_target`, the outline it morphs to: a kind's name (`"Star"`), the kind with its numbers (`{"kind": "Polygon", "size": [380, 380], "sides": 3}`; `corner` for a rectangle, `inner_ratio` for a star), or another shape layer beside it (`"@flower"`); then `shape.morph` moving from 0 to 100, in the same save. A chain (a circle, a square, a triangle) is one layer a stretch, each morphing to the next.
- **Type weight:** `font.weight` takes the nine weights of type, `Thin`, `ExtraLight`, `Light`, `Regular`, `Medium`, `SemiBold`, `Bold`, `ExtraBold`, `Black` (100 to 900); `font.catalog` says the weights each family has.
- **Something around a text or on a letter** (brackets, a frame, a line under it): never placed by numbers guessed from the font size. Place it on what the engine measures of the text, and look at the picture (`.refusion/look/`); the engine binding a shape to a text's letters is to come.

## The project's file

The project's folder holds `project.rfp`: this document, headed with `written`, the fingerprint of the engine's copy it was written from. The link is on unless the owner turned it off (`project.file_state` says `enabled`). Edit that file by hand and save it: the engine reads it once it stands still and applies what you changed in one history step, and answers every save, each file in a reply of its own beside it: `project.reply.json` for `project.rfp`, `scenes/<name>.reply.json` for a scene file (`applied`, `changes`, `errors`, `remarks`, `notes`, `answers`, the fingerprint of the save it answers, and `drawn`, the pictures of what changed; a save of the text already applied is answered `unchanged`, a file taken away `removed`; its lists are cut at 20, the rest counted, and every reply is whole in `.refusion/replies.jsonl`). `rpro file-reply --file <file>` waits for the reply to that very save and prints it (`--full`: the whole reply); `rpro watch --project <folder>` prints every reply as it comes; and the hook (the skill's, installed once, and the one in the folder's `.claude/settings.json`) gives it to Claude Code after each save. A project that is not open in the app is applied and saved on its own (`"closed": true`). With `errors` nothing is applied, and every one is there at once, where the file says it: its `line` and `column`, the `text` written there, its `code` and `words`, the names `allowed` where a name was refused, and a ready `fix` where the engine has one; mend those lines only. The file stays as you wrote it, comments (`//`, `/* */`) and trailing commas included: the engine writes in it only what the owner changed in the app, at that value's place and in your units, with `written` named again (`"file_changed": true` in the reply: read it again before your next edit). Look at the result before you say it is done.

- **To try a change first,** add `"plan": true` to the head and save: the reply says what would change and every refusal (with `"plan": true`), and nothing is applied. Take the line away and save again to apply it.
- **Your notes and drafts** go in `.refusion/work/` in the folder: the engine does not read it, and it does not travel with the project.
- **The pictures of what changed:** after each save the engine draws each unit it changed, a sheet of four moments over the time the unit is seen: a scene into `.refusion/look/scenes/<name>.jpg`, a layer of the project's own into `.refusion/look/layers/<key>.jpg` (a name that is not plain lower case gets four letters of its fingerprint, so no two meet on a disk that does not tell case apart). The reply names them in `drawn` once they are there; read them before you go on.
- **A scene in a file of its own:** `scenes/<name>.rfs` holds one scene, `{"refusion": "scene", "version": 2, "name": ..., "canvas": [w, h], "for": ..., "layers": [...]}`, read and applied on its own with its own `written` and reply (an error in it waits alone). Put each new unit in its own file, written before the layer of `project.rfp` that shows it. A scene lives in one file: never move one out of `project.rfp`, nor rename a scene file; taking a scene file away takes its scene away.
- **Keep `written` as it is.** It names the copy your changes are measured against, so the owner's edits made since you read the file stay. A file whose `written` names no copy the engine keeps is built on the project as it is (the next line).
- **A value you and the owner both changed** is an error `conflict` at its line with its three `values` (`base`, `owner`, `file`). Ask the owner, then add `"on_conflict": "mine"` or `"theirs"` (or a map by address) and save again; it is used once.
- **To take a layer away, take its lines away** (a layer with a key; one with no key is not, `delete_needs_key`). **A value's line taken away** goes back to a new layer's default. **A scene's entry taken away** takes the scene away; the layers that show it go with their lines too (else `scene_in_use`). A line of the head or of a scene's own taken away stays (`field_removed`).
- **To rename a layer's key,** write the new `key` with `"was": "old_key"` beside it: the layer keeps all it has.
- **The head:** the project's length (`for`) is written in a project of scenes; a sequence is as long as its content (`length_follows_content`). The canvas size, the frame rate and the motion blur are the owner's (`header_owners`, `needs_owner`). A new scene is named by its name alone, never by an `id` (`scene_id_given`).
- **A file that names no kept copy** (no `written`, or one too old) is built on the project as it is: what it gives is set, what it leaves out stays, nothing is taken away, and `written` is put in its head (the reply says so).
- **Refused or unreadable, the file stays as you wrote it:** the engine does not write over it until you save it mended, or the owner lets it go. With the link off, nothing is applied (`file_link_off`): ask the owner to turn it on.
